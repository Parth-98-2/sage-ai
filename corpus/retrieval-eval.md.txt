# Sage Retrieval Evaluation Set

## Purpose

Evaluate whether Sage can retrieve the right source passages for questions that range from direct textual lookup to substantially reworded conceptual questions.

For each question, the expected passage/concept is a retrieval target, not an expected answer.

---

## Category 1 — Direct / Lexical

These should be relatively easy for PostgreSQL full-text search.

### Q1

What did Michael Bloomberg say about making five-year plans?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Tactical plans for the next few steps; adjust based on what happens; don't devise a five-year plan.

### Q2

What did Bloomberg say about getting fired from Solomon Brothers?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Being fired after 15 years; his reaction to it; moving forward rather than looking over his shoulder.

### Q3

What did Bloomberg say about acting instead of spending months planning?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** “Get action”; Bloomberg's company acted from day one while others planned for months.

### Q4

What did Napoleon believe about boldness when actually carrying out a decision?

**Expected source:** Napoleon
**Expected passage/concept:** Be cautious/timid while planning, but once the decision is made, act boldly and relentlessly.

### Q5

What did Napoleon say about having definitive plans?

**Expected source:** Napoleon
**Expected passage/concept:** He often had no definitive plans, but rather projects; circumstances forced him to change his mind.

---

## Category 2 — Reworded

These deliberately avoid some of the most obvious words from the relevant passages.

### Q6

How did Bloomberg approach the problem of building a business when the future could not be mapped out in advance?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Make small incremental advances, take tactical steps, observe reality, and adjust rather than constructing a comprehensive long-term scheme.

### Q7

If the situation changes after I have started pursuing an objective, what approach does the Napoleon material suggest?

**Expected source:** Napoleon
**Expected passage/concept:** Preserve the objective while changing the route; remain flexible in response to circumstances rather than stubbornly following the original path.

### Q8

What does Bloomberg's experience suggest about waiting until a new venture has been completely figured out before beginning?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Stop overanalyzing; start; build a practical vision around a real customer need; learn through action.

### Q9

According to the Napoleon material, how should preparation and execution differ?

**Expected source:** Napoleon
**Expected passage/concept:** Investigate and exaggerate possible dangers before deciding; after deciding, eliminate hesitation and commit fully.

### Q10

What does the Bloomberg material suggest about setting an ambitious destination without knowing the entire route?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Have a basic end goal but remain flexible about how to reach it; don't let long-range planning interfere with action.

---

## Category 3 — Semantic / Conceptual

These are the important tests. The wording intentionally does not closely mirror the source.

### Q11

I have a large objective, but I cannot predict the sequence of events that will eventually get me there. How should I think about the gap between the destination and the path?

**Expected source:** Napoleon
**Expected passage/concept:** Pursue projects rather than rigidly predetermined plans; circumstances may force changes; retain the objective while adapting the route.

**Difficulty:** High

### Q12

Suppose I spend six months designing a perfect strategy and then reality immediately invalidates several of its assumptions. What philosophy from these sources speaks most directly to that problem?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Reality determines what is possible; make small earned advances, look one move ahead, and adjust based on what actually happens.

**Difficulty:** High

### Q13

When does thinking become counterproductive rather than useful according to the material?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Overanalyzing and excessive planning can prevent starting; planning has a place, but should not become an obstacle to action.

**Secondary source:** Napoleon
**Secondary concept:** Once a decision has been made, hesitation is harmful and action should follow decisively.

**Difficulty:** High

### Q14

How can someone combine extreme ambition with the fact that they have very little control over future events?

**Expected source:** Napoleon
**Expected passage/concept:** Maintain immense ambition while accepting circumstances and adapting to them; flexible means can serve a firm purpose.

**Difficulty:** Very high

### Q15

Imagine two founders. One spends months constructing a detailed model of the next five years. The other chooses a direction, takes a small step, observes the result, and chooses the next step from there. What evidence in the corpus speaks to this difference?

**Expected source:** Bloomberg
**Expected passage/concept:** Small incremental advances; tactical plans; look one move ahead; adapt to reality; no five-year plan.

**Secondary source:** Bloomberg
**Secondary concept:** Others planned for months while Bloomberg's company acted from day one.

**Difficulty:** Very high

---

## Evaluation rule

For every question, record:

* Whether the expected passage appears in the top 1 result
* Whether it appears in the top 5
* Whether it appears in the top 10
* The rank of the best relevant passage
* Whether the result is genuinely relevant or only matches words
* Any obviously irrelevant high-ranking results

Do not change the retrieval implementation while running this evaluation.

The purpose is to establish a baseline for the current lexical retriever before introducing embeddings or hybrid retrieval.
